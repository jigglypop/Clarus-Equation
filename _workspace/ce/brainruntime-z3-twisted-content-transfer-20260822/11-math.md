## Definition

Within a predeclared finite family, normalized content rows obey

$$
y_{ab}=A_a+B_{(b+\kappa a)\bmod3},
\qquad \kappa\in\{1,2\}.
$$

The learner receives eight shuffled pairs of raw cue and current content sum,
plus the ninth cue without its content.  It enumerates additive Cartesian
charts from the raw cue equality alone.  The exact count `72` is an apparatus
property of this generic frozen cue fixture, not a theorem about arbitrary
3-by-3 cue collections.

For every chart and $k\in\{0,1,2\}$, define the signless incidence design

$$
(M_k)_{(a,b),a}=1,qquad
(M_k)_{(a,b),3+(b+ka)\bmod3}=1.
$$

The fitted component table and relative residual are

$$
\Theta_k=M_k^+Y,
\qquad
\rho_k=\frac{\|M_k\Theta_k-Y\|_F}{\max(\|Y\|_F,\epsilon)}.
$$

## Conditional identifiability

For every admitted candidate,

$$
\operatorname{rank}M_k
=\operatorname{rank}\begin{pmatrix}M_k\\e_q^\top\end{pmatrix}=5.
$$

The connected bipartite incidence graph leaves only the additive gauge
$A+c,B-c$.  Consequently $e_q^\top\Theta_k$ is identified inside this family.
All 36 zero-residual chart candidates produce the same query content to the
frozen tolerance.

The data do not distinguish $\kappa=1$ from $\kappa=2$: both appear in every
admissible chart/gauge orbit.  The identified object is therefore the
nonzero-twist equivalence class and its common query prediction, not the sign
or semantic identity of a hidden $\kappa$.

## Additive falsifier and complete no-go

The matched $k=0$ model is fitted with the same observed rows and component
capacity.  A positive residual separates it from the nonzero-twist orbit.
Nevertheless,

$$
y_q'=y_q+\delta,\qquad \delta\ne0,
$$

changes no observed row.  Thus the finite-family assumption is indispensable;
arbitrary interaction completion remains unidentifiable.
