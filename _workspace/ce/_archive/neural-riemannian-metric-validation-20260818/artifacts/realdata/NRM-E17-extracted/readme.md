# Tuft dendrites in frontal motor cortex enable flexible learning during decision-making tasks.

This repository contains behavioral, electrophysiological, functional imaging (two-photon imaging) and anatomical data that were used to generate figures in our paper "Tuft dendrites in frontal motor cortex enable flexible learning during decision-making tasks." by Maristany de las Casas et al., 2026 (doi: 10.1126/science.adx4358).

## Abstract

Flexible learning relies on integrating sensory and contextual information to adjust behavioral output in different environments. For licking tasks in rodents, the anterolateral motor cortex (ALM) is critical for action selection. Here, we show that inputs critical to decision making converge on L1 in ALM where they can influence the apical dendrites of layer 5 pyramidal neurons responsible for motor planning and execution. We therefore investigated their role in a rule-switching decision-making paradigm. Activation of dendrite-inhibiting layer 1 interneurons impaired relearning, without affecting learned behavior. Dendritic imaging and extracellular recordings revealed that this inhibition had profound effects on calcium activity and bursting. Further, excitatory synaptic inputs to tuft dendrites exhibited rule-dependent clustering. We conclude that apical dendrites are a key computational component of flexible learning.
