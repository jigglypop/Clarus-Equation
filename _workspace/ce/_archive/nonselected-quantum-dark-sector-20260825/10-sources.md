# Sources and empirical constraints

Status: COMPLETE

This file records source facts relevant to the proposed interpretation. The
links are primary papers or official collaboration releases where available.
No source below establishes that ordinary quantum mechanics makes unselected
histories gravitate.

## Quantum conditioning and the cross-branch limit

* Davies and Lewis, “An operational approach to quantum probability” (1970),
  [original paper record](https://scispace.com/papers/an-operational-approach-to-quantum-probability-31fcpwzbky).
  A quantum instrument assigns a completely positive operation to each
  recorded outcome; the outcome probability is its trace and the conditioned
  state is the normalized operation. The complement of a selected outcome is
  another coarse-grained operation, not an automatically existing gravitating
  substance. The latter sentence is an inference from the instrument
  formalism, not a quotation.
* Ozawa, “Quantum measuring processes of continuous observables” (1984),
  [paper record](https://cir.nii.ac.jp/crid/1363107369074760320). Measurement
  processes induce completely positive instruments and state changes. This
  supports defining a nonselected operation, but supplies no stress tensor or
  coupling to Einstein gravity.
* In standard decoherence, alternatives become approximately noninterfering
  components of a state; neither the Davies–Lewis instrument nor decoherence
  by itself adds a second classical source in the selected branch. Therefore
  “unselected paths gravitate” must remain a new physical-map axiom unless a
  microscopic gravity model is supplied.

## Covariant scalar sector

* For the action in the contract, metric variation gives
  (T_{\mu\nu}=\nabla_\mu\phi\nabla_\nu\phi-g_{\mu\nu}[\frac12(\nabla\phi)^2+V(\phi)]).
  Diffeomorphism invariance plus the Klein–Gordon equation implies
  \(\nabla^\mu T_{\mu\nu}=0\). This is a theorem of the declared EFT, not an
  empirical input. With an interaction (Q_\nu) to another sector, only the
  total stress tensor is conserved and \(\nabla^\mu T^{(\phi)}_{\mu\nu}=Q_\nu\).
* Wald, “The back reaction effect in particle creation in curved spacetime”
  (1977), and Hollands–Wald, “Conservation of the stress tensor in
  perturbative interacting quantum field theory in curved spacetimes” (2002),
  [bibliographic record](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/1967891_CONSERVATION_OF_THE_STRESS_TENSOR_IN_PERTURBATIVE_INTERACTING_QUANTUM_FIELD_THEORY_IN_CURVED_SPACETIMES),
  establish the importance of covariant renormalization/conservation in QFT
  on curved backgrounds. They do not identify measurement complements with
  a new classical field.

## Oscillating scalar as matter and its limitations

* A homogeneous massive scalar with \(m\gg H\) has rapid oscillations,
  \(\langle p\rangle\simeq0\), \(\langle\rho\rangle\propto a^{-3}\), after
  averaging over many periods. This follows directly from the KG equation
  and virial averaging and is conditional on the EFT initial state.
* Hui, Ostriker, Tremaine and Witten, “Ultralight scalars as cosmological dark
  matter,” Phys. Rev. D 95, 043541 (2017),
  [APS](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevD.95.043541), finds wave-pressure
  suppression: halos below approximately
  (10^7(m/10^{-22}{\rm eV})^{-3/2}M_\odot) do not form, with substantial
  suppression below a few (10^{10}(m/10^{-22}{\rm eV})^{-4/3}M_\odot).
  Thus CDM-like behavior additionally requires de Broglie/Jeans scales small
  enough not to erase observed structure; the \(m\gg H\) background condition
  alone is insufficient.
* These are falsifiers for a residual scalar: CMB and Lyman-\(\alpha\) power,
  dwarf/halo abundance, lensing and galaxy clustering constrain the mass and
  fraction. A claimed unified sector must specify its fraction and transfer
  function before comparison.

## Vacuum-like component and current observations

* A constant offset has (T^{(\Lambda)}_{\mu\nu}=-V_\Lambda g_{\mu\nu}),
  \(\rho_\Lambda=V_\Lambda\), \(p_\Lambda=-V_\Lambda\), hence \(w=-1\).
  This is an identity within the action; it does not predict the observed
  magnitude of \(V_\Lambda\).
* DESI Collaboration, “DESI 2024: Constraints on Physics-Focused Aspects of
  Dark Energy using DESI DR1 BAO Data” (22 May 2024),
  [arXiv:2405.13588](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.13588), reports that thawing,
  emergent and mirage dynamical classes fit DESI DR1+ CMB+SN at least as well
  as \(\Lambda\)CDM, with reported improvements \(\Delta\chi^2\approx-5\) to
  \(-17\) for one extra parameter. This is model/data-combination dependent,
  not evidence for the residual-path interpretation.
* DESI Collaboration full-shape analysis (2024),
  [arXiv:2411.12022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.12022), gives, in a flat
  \(\Lambda\)CDM combination with CMB, \(\Omega_m=0.3056\pm0.0049\) and
  \(\sigma_8=0.8121\pm0.0053\); DESI+ CMB+DESY3 gives
  \(H_0=68.40\pm0.27\) km s\(^{-1}\) Mpc\(^{-1}\). Its modified-gravity
  parameters are consistent with GR. These are benchmark constraints for any
  new stress tensor/coupling.

## Strongest counterexamples and required tests

1. Instrument conditioning is normalized within the selected algebra; it does
   not conserve a discarded probability as an extra energy component. Hence the
   literal parent claim is not a consequence of standard measurement theory.
2. A free scalar can supply both matter-like oscillations and a vacuum offset,
   but the amplitude, mass, offset and map from nonselected histories are
   independent inputs. The split is not identifiable from one abundance number.
3. Ultralight-wave pressure provides a direct falsifier: masses/fractions that
   erase the observed small-scale power are excluded, even if the background
   equation of state is dust-like.
4. Dynamical-dark-energy fits remain statistically and systematically dependent
   on BAO, CMB and supernova combinations; a constant \(V_\Lambda\) must be
   tested against expansion history and growth rather than inferred from a
   numerical coincidence.
