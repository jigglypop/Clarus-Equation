# Source lane

## Established observations

- Foster et al., *Nature* (2021), DOI
  [10.1038/s41586-021-03993-3](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-021-03993-3):
  cortico–basal-ganglia–thalamic pathways show both convergence and partial
  segregation; direct and indirect routes can reconverge on SNr neurons.
- Cui et al., *Nature* (2013), DOI
  [10.1038/nature11846](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4039389/):
  direct- and indirect-pathway striatal neurons are concurrently active during
  action initiation. A strict Go/NoGo binary tree is therefore inadequate.
- Nambu et al. (2002), DOI
  [10.1016/S0168-0102(02)00027-5](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12067746/):
  the cortex–STN–pallidum hyperdirect route provides a short-latency pathway.
- Plenz and Kitai, *Nature* (1999), DOI
  [10.1038/23281](https://www.nature.com/articles/23281): STN–GPe forms an
  excitatory–inhibitory recurrent loop, so the anatomical circuit is not a DAG.
- Abdi et al., *J Neurosci* (2015), DOI
  [10.1523/JNEUROSCI.4662-14.2015](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25926446/):
  GPe cell classes provide distinct feedback toward STN and striatum.
- Haber et al., *J Neurosci* (2000), DOI
  [10.1523/JNEUROSCI.20-06-02369.2000](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10704511/):
  ascending striatonigrostriatal spirals support asymmetric cross-loop influence,
  not a single fixed rooted tree.
- Hollerman and Schultz, *Nature Neuroscience* (1998),
  [source](https://www.nature.com/articles/nn0898_304): dopamine neurons report
  temporal reward-prediction errors during learning.

## Status boundary

The sources support recurrent, convergent/divergent, partially hierarchical
action-control circuits. They do not establish an inverse tree, XGBoost, a
unique parent for each action, or a fixed-depth hierarchy in the brain.
